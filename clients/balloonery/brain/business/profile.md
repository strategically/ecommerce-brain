# Balloonery: business profile

_Facts only, each with source and date. Calls cover 2025-12-09 to 2026-11-19 (22 notes, 2 of them failed recordings and 2 with no discussion recorded). Currency is not stated on most dollar figures; the client register currency is CAD (see open-questions)._

## What the business is
- Shopify store selling helium-filled balloon bouquets and larger balloon decor, delivered through local partners in several cities (calls 2025-12-09, 2026-02-12, 2026-11-05). Markets named: Toronto (Canada), New York, Chicago and Houston (calls 2026-02-12, 2026-07-30, 2026-08-12).
- The default store is the Canadian store, so visitors are redirected towards it by default (call 2026-01-15). Canada has thousands of SKUs; the US range was intentionally cut (call 2026-03-26; count conflicts, see open-questions).
- The catalogue changes as manufacturers change SKUs and products sell out; seasonal collections are re-enabled rather than rebuilt (call 2025-12-09).
- Part of the product component data sits in a partner app, not in Shopify; partner-app component lists per bouquet are treated as more reliable than AI-guessed components (calls 2025-12-09, 2026-01-15). Shopify is the source of truth for attributes from 2026-05-07 (call 2026-05-07).
- Decor products are standard latex products that partners can hold as stock; bouquets are more specific (call 2026-11-05). Individual balloons are viewed as commodity products with the image as the differentiator in Shopping (Shawn, call 2026-08-25).
- Balloon market is fragmented: many small home-based sellers and a few larger e-commerce players (call 2026-09-10). Competitors bid on overlapping terms and on the Balloonery name (calls 2026-07-30, 2026-08-12).
- Imagery is described as weaker than competitors' and the mobile speed score as low (call 2026-07-30).

## Orders and customers
- Customers often order by phone or email through draft orders, particularly larger decor orders (calls 2025-12-09, 2026-03-12). Draft orders are the higher-ticket and more margin-rich orders (call 2026-03-12).
- Corporate buyers mainly buy standard products in larger quantities, often via draft orders (call 2025-12-09); corporate is a higher-value segment and a route to larger tickets (Shawn, call 2026-09-10).
- Decor is largely ordered through draft orders handled by customer service; an enquiry typically takes about a week to payment, some longer (call 2026-10-07). Phone enquiries arrive at a meaningful daily volume; many are simple orders from customers who do not order online (call 2026-10-07).
- Shopify is the record of orders; draft orders are created manually after phone enquiries and no CRM is in use (call 2026-10-07). The phone system keeps call records that are not linked to a lead or order (call 2026-10-07).
- A portion of gift-delivery orders are placed by customers outside the delivery location for someone in it (call 2026-11-05).
- Seasonality: peak April to June, taper in July, quiet August (call 2026-04-23); May and June busy, November good, December has the busiest two weeks then the quietest two, September usually larger than in 2026 (call 2026-08-25). Hanukkah demand is significant in Toronto and New York, less so in Chicago and Houston (call 2026-11-05). Loose ceiling balloons are a seasonal New Year's product (call 2026-05-07).
- Before AI work, fewer than half of products were explicit occasion types (call 2026-05-21).

## Partners and delivery model
- Product prices are set centrally; partners control the delivery price, set by zone (calls 2026-02-12, 2026-11-05). Partners asked for higher product prices to improve gross margin (call 2026-02-12).
- Partners are added in concentric bands around existing cities to increase density (call 2026-02-12). Partner selection is a custom zip-code assignment; overlapping zip codes route to the lower-priced partner (call 2026-03-12). Partners can change their own zip codes; frequent changes are discouraged because they affect the ad algorithm (call 2026-07-16).
- Balloonery pays partners at month end for the previous month's deliveries; Shopify payouts arrive about seven days after orders (calls 2026-03-12, 2026-09-10).
- Three partners as of 2026-07-16; Balloonery helps fund delivery fees for the Chicago partner; the other two are described as reliable and the New York partner's order handling as a concern (call 2026-07-16). New York has two partners serving different geographies (call 2026-09-10). A Houston partner exists and has asked how to scale (call 2026-09-10).
- Partners invest in an inventory set at onboarding; two models considered: Balloonery supplies minimal startup inventory for a 35 to 45 percent commission, or partners buy ongoing inventory through the Balloonery portal (call 2026-07-02). Not decided (see open-questions).
- Partner selection rules set 2026-07-02: urban downtown-core locations, minimum monthly order level per partner (call 2026-07-02).
- Balloonery has been building a partner-acquisition tool that scrapes balloon vendors and profiles each (call 2026-07-16); Shawn uses a headless CRM for partner acquisition (calls 2026-08-12, 2026-10-07).
- Toronto capacity constrains volume, so paid advertising is limited there; new markets start with no volume (call 2025-12-09). Toronto footprint covers about 20 to 25 surrounding cities and towns, with delivery rates that differ across the area (call 2026-07-30).
- Delivery fees shown at checkout are the main friction in building a new market (call 2026-09-10). Checkout blocks orders from zip codes outside the served area (call 2026-11-05).
- Decor delivery pricing already includes setup and is tiered by tear-down or no tear-down (call 2026-11-05).
